Rising now

Terms whose share of the corpus grew fastest, ranked by the lower bound of the estimate rather than the point estimate, with false-discovery control across every term screened. A term going from one paper to five is not a breakout.

When each idea broke out

Kleinberg's burst detection over the whole corpus. Each bar is a period when a term's share ran persistently above its own baseline — a dated interval, not a shape you have to squint at. Thickness is burst intensity; the label on the right is the field the term mostly belongs to. Terms whose field mix simply mirrors the corpus are excluded, which is what keeps academic filler off this list. Click a row to chart it.

The deadline fingerprint

Conference deadlines leave a signature in a field's submission pattern. Each shape is the month-of-year index for one category: the dashed circle is a month of average volume, and the lobes are the months that field actually posts.

How arXiv's composition shifted

Share of all submissions by top-level group. Computed with fractional attribution, so a paper listing three categories contributes one third to each and the shares sum to one.

Method & limits

Built from the arXiv metadata snapshot. Papers are bucketed by the v1 submission date, never the last-modified date. Category counts use any-listing attribution by default, matching arXiv's own cat: queries. Shares divide by the total papers in the same snapshot, never by a different source and never by the sum of whatever series you have selected.

Three things this data cannot tell you, stated plainly. Category membership is the current list, so a 2010 paper cross-listed into cs.LG in 2024 counts in 2010 — today's taxonomy is back-projected onto history. Titles and abstracts are the latest revision's, so a 2016 paper revised in 2025 can register a 2025 term in 2016. And arXiv is not science: preprinting is near-universal in high-energy physics and weak in chemistry and clinical medicine, so a cross-field comparison partly measures preprinting culture.
