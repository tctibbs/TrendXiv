Rising now

Each word's share of papers over the last 12 months, set against the three years before it. Rare words are pulled toward the average first, so one paper becoming five cannot outrank a real trend. Words are then ranked by the bottom of their likely range rather than the middle of it.

When each idea broke out

In any given month a word is either running at its usual rate or sitting in one of three raised states. The algorithm traces the cheapest path through those states across all 35 years, paying a penalty each time it steps up, so a bar appears only where a rise held long enough to be worth the jump. Thicker bars sat higher. The label on the right is the field the word belongs to, and clicking a row charts it.

The deadline fingerprint

Conference deadlines leave a mark on when a field posts. The dashed circle is an average month, and each lobe shows how far a given month runs above or below it. The more a field revolves around one annual deadline, the more lopsided its shape.

How this is built

A paper is counted in the month it was first posted, so revising an old paper cannot move it into the present. A paper counts in every field it is listed under, which is what arXiv's own cat: search does, so the numbers here should match what you find there. Shares always divide by the total papers in the same snapshot.

Some limits worth knowing. Fields are recorded as they stand today, so a 2010 paper added to machine learning in 2024 still counts as machine learning back in 2010. Titles and abstracts are the latest version, so a 2016 paper revised in 2025 can appear to use a 2025 word in 2016. And arXiv is not all of science. High energy physics posts nearly everything, chemistry and clinical medicine post very little, so comparing across fields partly measures posting habits.
